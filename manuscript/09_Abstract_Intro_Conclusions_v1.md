## Abstract (structured, 250 words; Medical Physics format)

**Purpose.** To compare the propagation of segmentation-mask perturbation through pretrained foundation-model (FM) embeddings versus IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomic features on a public lung-CT cohort, and to quantify the downstream impact of intraclass-correlation-coefficient (ICC) based stability filtering on malignancy classification.

**Methods.** We selected 310 LIDC-IDRI lung nodules (155 malignant + 155 benign, 227 unique patients, median pairwise inter-reader Dice 0.789) and generated up to 12 perturbation conditions per nodule (≤4 reader masks, 7 controlled morphological perturbations with a 25 %-volume-preservation guard, 1 Dice-calibrated synthetic mask). For each perturbation we extracted PyRadiomics features in matched 3D (107 features) and 2D (102 features) arms and BiomedCLIP embeddings (512 dimensions) from the max-area axial slice. We computed ICC(2,1) per feature/dimension across the ten universal perturbation conditions, tested four pre-registered hypotheses, and performed a pre-specified diameter-stratified subgroup analysis (6-10 mm, 10-20 mm, >20 mm).

**Results.** Both paradigms achieved comparable proportions of stable features at ICC ≥ 0.75 (PyRadiomics-2D 91.2 %, PyRadiomics-3D 94.4 %, BiomedCLIP 90.4 %; pairwise z-tests not significant) — H1 rejected. However, the ICC distribution shapes differed categorically (Kolmogorov-Smirnov D = 0.68-0.72, p < 10⁻³⁹): radiomics bimodal with 63-68 % excellent (ICC ≥ 0.90), BiomedCLIP unimodal-moderate with 90 % good (0.75 ≤ ICC < 0.90) and zero poor dimensions. Downstream AUC was unchanged by ICC filtering for both paradigms (H3 non-inferiority supported, margin 0.020). BiomedCLIP's calibration improved substantially under filtering (Brier 0.144 → 0.115; ECE 0.129 → 0.065). The apparent aggregate AUC advantage of radiomics (0.95) over BiomedCLIP (0.90) dissolved within size-matched strata.

**Conclusions.** FM embeddings and IBSI radiomics exhibit fundamentally different per-dimension stability profiles. Routine ICC-filtering provides no discrimination benefit but substantially improves FM-embedding calibration. Aggregate FM-vs-radiomics AUC comparisons are vulnerable to size-class confounding in benchmark cohorts.

**Keywords:** radiomics; foundation models; segmentation variability; reproducibility; lung CT; LIDC-IDRI; intraclass correlation coefficient; calibration.

## 1 Introduction (revised v2, replaces v1)

*Target: ~700 wds. Final length: ~720 wds. Replaces `05_Introduction_v1.md` to incorporate H1 rejection, H1' reframe, and size-confound finding.*

Quantitative image-derived biomarker extraction has bifurcated into two paradigms that the literature has only recently begun to compare directly. Handcrafted radiomics extracts hundreds of mathematically defined features — first-order intensity statistics, three-dimensional shape descriptors, and second-order texture metrics computed over grey-level co-occurrence, run-length, size-zone, dependence, and neighbourhood matrices — using Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative (IBSI) 1.0 reference implementations such as PyRadiomics, MIRP, LIFEx, or CERR [REF Lambin 2017; REF Zwanenburg 2020]. Pretrained foundation-model (FM) embeddings replace the engineered dictionary with a dense vector produced by a vision encoder pretrained at scale on biomedical or natural images, including BiomedCLIP (a Vision Transformer trained contrastively on 15 million biomedical image-text pairs), RadImageNet-ResNet50 (pretrained on 1.35 million radiology images), the MedSAM image-encoder, and a growing family of three-dimensional encoders [REF Zhang 2024; REF Mei 2022; REF Ma 2024; REF Pai 2024]. The implicit claim in much of the FM-radiomics literature is that FM embeddings offer both stronger discrimination and stronger robustness to the upstream image-processing variability — segmentation, scanner, reconstruction kernel — that has driven the radiomics community to standardise around routine intraclass-correlation-coefficient (ICC) filtering at ICC ≥ 0.75 before any downstream modelling [REF Pavic 2018; REF van Timmeren 2020].

Whether the FM stability claim holds for the specific perturbation mode that radiomics ICC-filtering exists to address — segmentation-mask variability — is unresolved in the peer-reviewed literature. The peer-reviewed precedent is Pai et al. 2024 (*Nature Machine Intelligence*) [REF Pai 2024], which introduced FMCIB — a contrastively pretrained three-dimensional ResNet50 — and reported ICC = 0.984 for its feature-based predictions on the RIDER same-day repeat-scan dataset together with significantly higher stability than a Supervised CNN baseline under fifty trials of three-dimensional Gaussian seed-point jitter (σ² = 16 voxels per dimension) on the LUNG1 cohort. Pai 2024 thus established that a well-pretrained 3D FM can be more stable than a supervised CNN under scanner-repeat and seed-point variability, but did not perturb the segmentation mask itself, did not include an IBSI-aligned handcrafted-radiomics comparator on the same lesions, did not report per-dimension ICC across the embedding, and did not report pre-versus-post stability-filter downstream task performance. A contemporaneous *Research Square* preprint by the same group extends the 2024 benchmark to ten 3D FMs using global cosine similarity but has not undergone peer review and is therefore cited here only for context. Cosma et al. 2025 [REF Cosma 2025] working independently on breast MRI for triple-negative breast cancer subtype prediction reported the counterintuitive finding that ICC-based filtering of handcrafted radiomic features can exclude features with predictive value — raising the possibility that the inherited ICC-filter convention may be unnecessary when modern regularised classifiers are used. The central question of the present work, unanswered in the peer-reviewed literature, is: how do FM embeddings behave when the segmentation mask itself is perturbed, and does ICC-based stability filtering produce a measurable downstream-task benefit when the same perturbation is applied to FM embeddings and to handcrafted radiomic features extracted from the same lesions?

We address this on the LIDC-IDRI lung-CT cohort, which uniquely provides up to four independent expert segmentations per nodule [REF Armato 2011]. For 310 balanced nodules selected for clear binary malignancy labels, we extract IBSI-aligned PyRadiomics features under matched two-dimensional (central-slice) and three-dimensional (full-volume) configurations and BiomedCLIP embeddings on the same central axial slice. For each nodule we apply four natural inter-reader perturbations together with seven controlled morphological perturbations and one Dice-calibrated synthetic mask. We test four pre-registered hypotheses: that FM embeddings exhibit a higher proportion of stable features than radiomics under perturbation (H1); that downstream AUC degradation under ICC-based stability filtering is smaller for FM embeddings (H2); that post-filter AUC is non-inferior to pre-filter AUC at margin 0.020 (H3); and that the highest-ICC FM dimensions concentrate a disproportionate share of the malignancy-classification weight (H4). We complement the pre-registered tests with a Kolmogorov-Smirnov comparison of ICC distribution shapes (pre-specified before any analysis run) and a diameter-stratified subgroup analysis as the pre-specified mitigation of the LIDC size-malignancy confound. The findings reframe several common assumptions about FM-vs-radiomics stability and have direct methodological implications for the design of future foundation-model-versus-radiomics benchmarking studies.

## 5 Conclusions

*Target: ~150 wds. Final length: ~165 wds.*

This study compared the propagation of segmentation-mask perturbation through pretrained BiomedCLIP embeddings and IBSI-aligned PyRadiomics features in matched 2D and 3D configurations on the public LIDC-IDRI lung-CT cohort. Both paradigms achieved comparable aggregate proportions of stable features under perturbation, but their per-feature ICC distributions differed categorically: handcrafted radiomics was bimodal with a large excellent-stratum core and a small unstable tail, while BiomedCLIP was unimodal-moderate with no catastrophically unstable dimensions. Routine ICC-based stability filtering provided no discrimination benefit for either paradigm but substantially improved BiomedCLIP's probability calibration. The apparent aggregate AUC superiority of handcrafted radiomics over BiomedCLIP was largely attributable to a structural size-malignancy confound in the cohort and dissolved within matched-size strata. We recommend that future foundation-model-versus-radiomics benchmarks report per-dimension stability metrics, size-stratified AUC, and calibration alongside discrimination, and that ICC-based dimension selection be considered for foundation-model deployment pipelines that report probability outputs.
