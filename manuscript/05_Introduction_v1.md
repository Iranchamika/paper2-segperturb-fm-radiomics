## 1. Introduction

Quantitative image-based biomarker extraction has bifurcated into two paradigms that the literature is only now beginning to compare head-to-head. Handcrafted radiomics produces hundreds of mathematically defined features — first-order intensity statistics, shape descriptors, and second-order texture metrics from Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative-aligned matrices including the grey-level co-occurrence, grey-level run-length, and grey-level size-zone matrices — extracted from a defined region of interest by deterministic algorithms whose IBSI 1.0 reference implementations (PyRadiomics, MIRP, CERR, LIFEx) are mutually validated to within rounding error [Lambin 2017; Zwanenburg 2020]. Pretrained foundation-model (FM) embeddings replace the engineered feature dictionary with a dense vector produced by a vision encoder pretrained at scale on biomedical or natural images: BiomedCLIP (Vision Transformer trained on 15 million biomedical image-text pairs from PubMed Central), RadImageNet (ResNet50 pretrained on 1.35 million radiology images across modalities), MedSAM image-encoder, and a growing family of three-dimensional encoders including FMCIB, ModelsGenesis, VISTA3D, and CT-FM [Mei 2022; Zhang 2024; Ma 2024]. The promise of FM embeddings is twofold: stronger task discrimination because the encoder has seen orders of magnitude more images than any single radiomics cohort, and, perhaps more importantly for clinical deployment, increased robustness to the upstream image-processing variability — scanner, reconstruction kernel, contour drift — that has driven the handcrafted-radiomics community to standardise around the routine practice of intraclass-correlation-coefficient filtering at ICC ≥ 0.75 before any downstream modelling [Pavic 2018; van Timmeren 2020].

Whether that routine ICC filter remains necessary in the FM era is unresolved. The peer-reviewed precedent is Pai et al. (2024) [*Nature Machine Intelligence*], which introduced FMCIB — a contrastively pretrained 3D ResNet50 — and reported ICC = 0.984 for FMCIB feature-based predictions on RIDER same-day repeat scans (26 patients, 15-min interval) and significantly higher stability than a Supervised CNN baseline under 50-trial three-dimensional Gaussian seed-point jitter at σ² = 16 voxels per dimension on the LUNG1 cohort. Pai 2024 thus establishes that a well-pretrained 3D FM can be more stable than a supervised CNN under scanner-repeat and seed-point variability — but it does not compare against IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomics, does not perturb the segmentation mask itself, does not report per-dimension ICC across the embedding, and does not include pre-vs-post stability-filter downstream-task analysis. A 2025 *Research Square* preprint by the same group (Pai et al. 2025, TumorImagingBench) extends the benchmark to ten 3D FMs using cosine similarity but does not yet address these gaps and has not undergone peer review. Cosma et al. (2025), working independently on breast MRI for triple-negative breast cancer subtype prediction, report the counter-intuitive finding that ICC-based stability filtering of handcrafted radiomic features can *exclude* features with predictive value [Cosma 2025], raising the possibility that the inherited ICC-filter convention may not in fact improve downstream performance when modern regularised classifiers are used. The question that motivates this work is therefore unanswered in the peer-reviewed literature: how do FM embeddings behave when the segmentation mask itself is perturbed, and does ICC-based stability filtering produce a measurable downstream-task benefit when the same perturbation is applied to FM embeddings and to handcrafted radiomic features extracted from the same lesions?

We address this gap with a controlled-perturbation study on the public Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC-IDRI) lung-CT cohort, which uniquely provides up to four independent expert segmentations per nodule [Armato 2011]. For ≥400 nodules selected for clear binary malignancy labels (200 benign, 200 malignant), we extract: PyRadiomics features under matched 2D (central-slice) and 3D (full-volume) configurations, and FM embeddings from BiomedCLIP and RadImageNet-ResNet50, both on the central axial slice. For each nodule we apply the four natural inter-reader perturbations and a controlled bank of seven morphological perturbations (erosion radius 1-3 voxels, dilation radius 1-2 voxels, translation by 1-2 voxels) plus one synthetic mask whose Dice against the consensus matches the empirical inter-reader Dice for that nodule. Per-feature and per-dimension ICC(2,1) is computed across all available perturbation conditions, and downstream malignancy classification under nested 5-fold cross-validation is reported with and without ICC-based feature filtering at four thresholds.

Four pre-specified hypotheses follow: H1, FM embeddings have a higher proportion of stable (ICC ≥ 0.75) dimensions than IBSI-aligned radiomic features extracted from the same nodules; H2, the downstream AUC degradation under ICC filtering is smaller for FM embeddings than for radiomics; H3, the Cosma 2025 finding generalises to lung CT and FM embeddings, with post-filter AUC non-inferior to pre-filter AUC at a 0.020 margin; H4, the highest-ICC FM dimensions concentrate a disproportionate share of the linear-probe weight for malignancy classification, supporting a "stable-informative core" view of FM representational structure.
