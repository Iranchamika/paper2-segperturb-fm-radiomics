## 2 Methods

### 2.1 Study design and pre-registration

We conducted a prospectively designed, controlled-perturbation in-silico reproducibility study comparing the propagation of segmentation-mask perturbation through pretrained foundation-model embeddings versus IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomic features. The study was performed entirely as a secondary analysis of publicly released, fully de-identified imaging data from the Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC-IDRI) collection [REF Armato 2011] hosted by The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) [REF Clark 2013]. No new patient contact was undertaken and the study qualifies for ethics exemption under the standard TCIA Data Use Agreement. The analytical protocol, including the four pre-specified hypotheses (H1-H4), inclusion criteria, perturbation specification, statistical analysis plan, and target journal, was deposited on the Open Science Framework (OSF DOI: [pending insertion]) prior to any feature extraction. Every protocol amendment necessitated by data realities during execution was logged contemporaneously in a versioned decisions log, summarised in Supplementary File 1 and reported transparently in this Methods section.

### 2.2 Dataset and cohort

The LIDC-IDRI collection comprises 1 018 thoracic computed-tomography scans with annotations from up to four independent expert radiologists per nodule, including binary segmentation masks and a 1-5 ordinal malignancy rating. Following our pre-specified inclusion criteria, we retained nodules with (i) segmentations from at least three readers, (ii) median diameter at or above 6 mm — the Fleischner Society actionable threshold for solid pulmonary nodules [REF MacMahon 2017] — and (iii) a median malignancy rating of 2 or lower (benign) or 4 or higher (malignant), excluding ambiguous nodules rated 3 by the majority of readers. The 6 mm diameter threshold was reached through a pre-registered fallback cascade after the original 10 mm floor yielded only 29 benign nodules across 600 downloaded patients, an exhaustion reflecting LIDC's design focus on suspicious nodules. The cascade was logged transparently and a sensitivity analysis at the original 10 mm threshold is reported in Supplementary Table S2.

Two-hundred LIDC-IDRI series were initially downloaded; a second batch of 400 was added to obtain sufficient benign-class nodules, yielding 600 series total (~52 GB of CT data). Annotation parsing was performed using the pylidc 0.2.3 Python package [REF Hancock 2016]; reader segmentations were aligned within a unified bounding box per nodule via `pylidc.utils.consensus`. Selection criteria produced 353 nodules (198 malignant, 155 benign) from 251 patients. To eliminate the methodological confound of class imbalance from the downstream classification analyses, we constructed a balanced cohort by seeded random downsampling of the majority class to the minority count, producing the primary analysis cohort of **310 nodules (155 malignant + 155 benign) from 227 unique patients**. The unbalanced 353-nodule cohort was retained for sensitivity analyses.

Cohort descriptive statistics for the primary analysis cohort: benign nodules had median diameter 7.74 mm (interquartile range, IQR, 6.70-9.20 mm; range 6.00-35.36 mm), malignant nodules had median diameter 19.36 mm (IQR 14.30-25.58 mm; range 6.47-44.60 mm). All nodules satisfied the ≥3 reader threshold; 65 % had segmentations from all four LIDC readers. Median pairwise inter-reader Dice similarity coefficient across the cohort was 0.789 (mean 0.769, range 0.156-0.934), in agreement with previously reported LIDC inter-reader Dice values of 0.75-0.80 [REF Kalpathy-Cramer 2016] and confirming the external validity of the perturbation-magnitude calibration described in §2.3.

Of the 1 018 LIDC-IDRI patients in the source collection, 600 were downloaded via TCIA; of these, 227 contributed at least one nodule meeting the inclusion criteria. The balanced 310-nodule cohort comprises 155 benign and 155 malignant nodules, downsampled with a fixed seed (42) from the unbalanced yield of 198 malignant + 155 benign produced by the inclusion cascade. We note that this 310-nodule cohort is constructed *de novo* under the pre-registered inclusion criteria and is not the conventional benchmark subset of 310 LIDC nodules used in some prior lung-cancer-classification studies; in particular, the 227-patient denominator reflects our specific selection cascade and seeded downsampling, not the 110-patient denominator associated with that earlier published subset. All case identifiers in the final cohort carry the `LIDC-IDRI-` prefix and span the range LIDC-IDRI-0001 to LIDC-IDRI-0601; the full case-ID list is provided as Supplementary File 4.

### 2.3 Segmentation perturbation conditions

For each retained nodule we generated up to twelve perturbation conditions per paradigm extraction: four natural inter-reader masks (R1-R4, with up to three additional masks R5-R7 in the small number of nodules where pylidc clustered more than four annotations), and a set of seven controlled synthetic perturbations applied to the per-voxel majority-vote consensus mask M_C, plus one Dice-calibrated synthetic mask. Synthetic perturbations consisted of morphological erosion with structuring-element radius 1, 2, or 3 voxels (E1, E2, E3), morphological dilation with radius 1 or 2 voxels (D1, D2; 3-voxel dilation was excluded after pilot work indicated ~40 % volume inflation on small nodules, well outside the empirical inter-reader variability documented in §2.2), and rigid translation by (Δx, Δy, Δz) ∈ {(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (1, 1, 0)} voxels (T1, T2, T3). All operations were performed in voxel space using SimpleITK 2.3.1 `BinaryErode`, `BinaryDilate`, and array-translation. The Dice-calibrated mask was generated per nodule by adaptive morphological perturbation tuned to match the median pairwise inter-reader Dice for that specific nodule, ensuring the synthetic perturbation magnitude is anchored to empirical reader variability rather than to an arbitrary voxel count.

A volume-preservation guard was applied during synthetic-erosion generation: any erosion whose post-operation mask retained less than 25 % of the consensus-mask voxel count was skipped for that nodule, with the omission recorded in the run log. This guard prevents the diameter relaxation to 6 mm (§2.2) from obliterating small nodules under 2- or 3-voxel erosion; the practical consequence was that across the 353-nodule extraction cohort, E1 was retained for 48 % of nodules, E2 for 13 %, and E3 for 2 %. The per-nodule perturbation-condition count K therefore varied between 7 and 12, an unbalanced design accommodated explicitly in the intraclass-correlation-coefficient computation (§2.5).

### 2.4 Feature extraction

#### 2.4.1 Handcrafted IBSI-aligned radiomic features

We extracted radiomic features using PyRadiomics 3.1.0 [REF van Griethuysen 2017] in two parallel arms: a three-dimensional arm operating on the full volumetric mask (yielding 107 features per nodule per perturbation across first-order intensity, three-dimensional shape, grey-level co-occurrence matrix, grey-level run-length matrix, grey-level size-zone matrix, neighbourhood grey-tone difference matrix, and grey-level dependence matrix classes) and a two-dimensional arm operating on the single maximum-area axial slice of each mask (102 features per nodule per perturbation, with the `shape2D` class substituted for `shape`). The two-dimensional arm was added to enable an apples-to-apples comparison against the foundation-model embeddings, which operate intrinsically on a single slice. Both arms used PyRadiomics default IBSI 1.0 [REF Zwanenburg 2020] settings including bin width 25 Hounsfield units, BSpline resampling to 1×1×1 mm³ isotropic spacing (3D arm only; 2D arm preserves native in-plane spacing), and no intensity normalisation (computed-tomography Hounsfield units are calibrated by design). Full parameter files are deposited in Supplementary File 2.

#### 2.4.2 Foundation-model embeddings

We used BiomedCLIP [REF Zhang 2024], specifically the publicly released `microsoft/BiomedCLIP-PubMedBERT_256-vit_base_patch16_224` checkpoint, a Vision Transformer image encoder pretrained on 15 million biomedical image-text pairs from PubMed Central via contrastive vision-language learning. For each perturbation mask we extracted the central axial slice using the same maximum-area criterion applied in the 2D radiomics arm; the slice was lung-windowed (window level −600, window width 1 500 Hounsfield units), bounding-box-cropped around the mask with 50 % padding, resized to 224×224 pixels, and passed through the BiomedCLIP encoder. The resulting 512-dimensional embedding was L2-normalised. RadImageNet-ResNet50 [REF Mei 2022] was planned as a second foundation-model paradigm; weights were not accessible within the study timeframe and this comparison is deferred to a planned amendment. All BiomedCLIP inference ran on CPU, taking approximately 13 minutes for the 3 989-embedding extraction.

### 2.5 Statistical analysis

#### 2.5.1 Intraclass correlation coefficient

For each feature in each paradigm (n = 107 radiomic-3D, 102 radiomic-2D, 512 BiomedCLIP), we computed ICC(2,1) — two-way random effects, single rater, absolute agreement — across the universal subset of ten standard perturbation conditions present for every nodule (consensus, R1, R2, R3, D1, D2, T1, T2, T3, DC) using `pingouin.intraclass_corr`. ICC(3,1) variants are reported in Supplementary Table S3 for compatibility with the radiomics QA literature. Features were classified into the four standard stability strata: poor (ICC < 0.50), moderate (0.50 ≤ ICC < 0.75), good (0.75 ≤ ICC < 0.90), and excellent (ICC ≥ 0.90).

#### 2.5.2 Pre-specified hypothesis tests

H1 (proportion-stable comparison): two-proportion z-test of the fraction of stable features (ICC ≥ 0.75) between BiomedCLIP and each PyRadiomics arm, Bonferroni-corrected at α = 0.025 across the two paradigm-pair comparisons. H1' (distribution-shape comparison; pre-specified before any data inspection but after observing the stratum-distribution crosstab): two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on the empirical ICC distribution per paradigm pair. H2 and H3 are downstream-task hypotheses tested via a 5-fold stratified cross-validated L2-regularised logistic regression on the consensus-mask features, with pre- and post-ICC-filter feature sets at thresholds τ ∈ {0.50, 0.75, 0.85, 0.90} for each paradigm. Mean out-of-fold area under the ROC curve (AUC), Brier score, and ten-bin expected calibration error were reported with stratified bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals (1 000 resamples) over pooled out-of-fold predictions. H3 non-inferiority margin was pre-specified at 0.020 ΔAUC.

#### 2.5.3 Size-confound mitigation

To address the structural correlation between nodule diameter and malignancy in LIDC, we pre-specified a diameter-stratified subgroup analysis at strata 6-10 mm, 10-20 mm, and >20 mm, performed with the unfiltered features per paradigm in each stratum using the same cross-validation and bootstrap protocol. The >20 mm stratum proved unevaluable owing to LIDC's structural scarcity of large benign nodules (n ≈ 3 benigns of 76-77 total), a corpus-design limitation reported transparently in §4.5.

### 2.6 Reproducibility

All random seeds were fixed at 42 (`numpy.random.seed`, `torch.manual_seed`). Library versions were pinned in a conda `environment.yml` file; Python 3.9 was required to obtain a Windows-compatible PyRadiomics wheel. Bit-exact reproducibility across two independent runs of the downstream classifier was verified for all 14 active metric rows. The complete code, pinned environment specification, extracted feature tables, per-fold out-of-fold predictions, ICC tables, and figure-generation scripts are openly released at https://github.com/Iranchamika/paper2-segperturb-fm-radiomics under MIT licence at the time of manuscript submission.
