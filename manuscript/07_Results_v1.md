## 3 Results

### 3.1 Cohort

A total of 1 018 LIDC-IDRI patient series were screened via the pylidc-clustered annotation interface; 600 patient series were downloaded from TCIA across two batches and 353 annotated nodules met the pre-specified inclusion criteria (≥3 reader segmentations, median diameter ≥6 mm, median malignancy rating ≤2 or ≥4). Following balanced random downsampling of the malignant class, the primary analysis cohort comprised **310 nodules (155 malignant + 155 benign) drawn from 227 unique patients**. Benign nodules clustered toward the lower diameter range (median 7.74 mm, IQR 6.70-9.20 mm), while malignant nodules spanned a wider distribution toward larger sizes (median 19.36 mm, IQR 14.30-25.58 mm); this size-class asymmetry is a structural property of LIDC-IDRI and is addressed quantitatively in §3.5. Of the 310 cohort nodules, 65 % carried segmentations from all four LIDC readers and 35 % from three. The median pairwise inter-reader Dice similarity coefficient was 0.789 (mean 0.769, range 0.156-0.934), consistent with previously published LIDC inter-reader values (0.75-0.80) and supporting the external validity of the perturbation-magnitude calibration. Per-nodule perturbation-condition count K ranged from 7 to 12 across the 353-nodule extraction superset, reflecting the volume-preservation guard's intentional skipping of erosions that would have retained less than 25 % of the consensus-mask voxel count. The retention rate was 48 % for E1, 13 % for E2, and 2 % for E3, with all dilation, translation, and Dice-calibrated perturbations retained for every nodule. Example perturbation masks across the 8 mm to 23 mm diameter range are shown in Figure 1.

### 3.2 Per-feature stability under segmentation perturbation (H1)

Across the ten universal perturbation conditions, both handcrafted radiomics and BiomedCLIP embeddings achieved high proportions of features classified as stable (ICC(2,1) ≥ 0.75): 91.2 % for PyRadiomics-2D (93 of 102 features), 94.4 % for PyRadiomics-3D (101 of 107), and 90.4 % for BiomedCLIP (463 of 512 dimensions). Pre-registered two-proportion z-tests of the BiomedCLIP-vs-radiomics contrasts found no statistically significant difference under Bonferroni-corrected α = 0.025: BiomedCLIP vs PyRadiomics-2D Δ = -0.8 percentage points, z = -0.25, p = 0.80; BiomedCLIP vs PyRadiomics-3D Δ = -4.0 pp, z = -1.32, p = 0.19. **Hypothesis H1, as pre-registered, is therefore rejected** — pretrained foundation-model embeddings are not, on aggregate, more stable than IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomic features under segmentation-mask perturbation.

### 3.3 Distribution-shape divergence (H1')

The aggregate-proportion analysis obscures a qualitative divergence between paradigms that became visually apparent in the ICC stratum crosstab (Figure 2a) and the empirical cumulative distribution functions (Figure 2b). PyRadiomics exhibited a bimodal stability profile: 62.6 % of 3D features and 67.6 % of 2D features fell into the excellent stratum (ICC ≥ 0.90), with the remainder distributed across moderate, good, and a small unstable tail (one feature in each radiomics arm with ICC < 0.50). BiomedCLIP exhibited a unimodal moderate-stability profile: only 1 of 512 dimensions reached the excellent stratum, 90.2 % concentrated in the good stratum (0.75 ≤ ICC < 0.90), and **zero dimensions** fell into the poor stratum. The pre-specified two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests confirmed the two paradigms behave as categorically different populations of features: BiomedCLIP vs PyRadiomics-2D D = 0.722, p = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴⁴; BiomedCLIP vs PyRadiomics-3D D = 0.679, p = 4.3 × 10⁻⁴⁰. A secondary comparison between the two PyRadiomics arms found a small but statistically significant difference (D = 0.246, p = 0.003), with the 2D arm sitting marginally closer to the excellent stratum than the 3D arm. Hypothesis H1' (distribution-shape divergence between paradigms) is therefore strongly supported and is reported as the primary novel finding of this study.

### 3.4 Downstream classification and the effect of stability filtering (H2, H3)

Out-of-fold 5-fold stratified cross-validated L2-logistic-regression classifiers achieved area under the ROC curve values of 0.948 (95 % bootstrap CI 0.920-0.972) for PyRadiomics-3D, 0.955 (0.929-0.976) for PyRadiomics-2D, and 0.898 (0.861-0.931) for BiomedCLIP using the full unfiltered feature set per paradigm (Figure 3 and Table 2). Pre-vs-post ICC-filtered analyses produced two unexpected findings. **Hypothesis H2 — that FM embeddings would exhibit smaller AUC degradation than radiomics under stability filtering — was rejected** because both paradigms showed essentially zero AUC change under standard filtering (PyRadiomics-2D ΔAUC = +0.003 at icc≥0.75; BiomedCLIP ΔAUC = -0.002). **Hypothesis H3 — that post-filter AUC would be non-inferior to pre-filter AUC at margin 0.020 — was strongly supported for both paradigms.** PyRadiomics-2D AUC remained within ±0.003 of unfiltered at every filter threshold; BiomedCLIP AUC increased by +0.014 at the stricter ICC ≥ 0.85 threshold despite an 84 % reduction in the active feature set (512 → 84 dimensions). This generalises the Cosma et al. 2025 finding [REF Cosma 2025] — that ICC-based filtering can be unnecessary in modern radiomic pipelines — to lung-CT data and to foundation-model embeddings.

The calibration metrics revealed an additional unanticipated finding. BiomedCLIP's unfiltered predictions were poorly calibrated (Brier score 0.144, expected calibration error 0.129), substantially worse than either PyRadiomics arm (Brier 0.074-0.077, ECE 0.030-0.057). Filtering BiomedCLIP to dimensions with ICC ≥ 0.85 reduced the Brier score by 20 % (0.144 → 0.115) and the expected calibration error by 50 % (0.129 → 0.065), with simultaneous improvement in discrimination (AUC 0.898 → 0.912). This calibration-improvement effect was unique to BiomedCLIP and is consistent with an interpretation in which the moderately-stable BiomedCLIP dimensions encode perturbation-driven noise that adds uncertainty to the probability output without contributing malignancy signal.

### 3.5 Size-stratified subgroup analysis

The structural correlation between nodule diameter and malignancy in LIDC (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.7 in our cohort; 94 % of 6-10 mm nodules benign vs 97 % of >20 mm nodules malignant) motivated a pre-specified diameter-stratified subgroup analysis. Within the 6-10 mm stratum (n = 134; 6 % malignant) all three paradigms achieved statistically indistinguishable AUC values: 0.762 (95 % CI 0.535-0.917) for PyRadiomics-3D, 0.774 (0.638-0.896) for PyRadiomics-2D, and 0.758 (0.611-0.899) for BiomedCLIP (Figure 4, left panel). Within the 10-20 mm stratum (n = 99; 74 % malignant), PyRadiomics-3D maintained a 0.095 AUC lead over BiomedCLIP (0.889 vs 0.794), though the confidence intervals overlapped (radiomics-3D 0.805-0.960 vs BiomedCLIP 0.697-0.884) and the difference was not formally significant at this sample size (Figure 4, middle panel). The >20 mm stratum was not evaluable because the LIDC corpus contained only three benign nodules in this size range, a structural consequence of Lung-RADS' clinical convention of treating >20 mm solid nodules as suspicious by definition (Figure 4, right panel).

**The aggregate AUC ranking (PyRadiomics 0.95 > BiomedCLIP 0.90) is therefore substantially attributable to a size-malignancy confound in the cohort, not to a paradigm-level feature-quality difference.** Within the matched-size 6-10 mm stratum the three paradigms converge to within ~0.02 AUC of each other; within the 10-20 mm stratum PyRadiomics-3D retains a modest advantage that is consistent with — but not proof of — a genuine paradigm-level feature-quality difference in mid-size nodules. The methodological implications for future foundation-model-vs-radiomics benchmarking studies are addressed in the Discussion §4.4.
