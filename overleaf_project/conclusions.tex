Neither of the two pretrained foundation-model paradigms tested (BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip}; RadImageNet ResNet50~\cite{mei2022radimagenet}) exceeded IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomic features on either perturbation stability or downstream malignancy discrimination in this LIDC-IDRI cohort, and the shape of the per-feature stability distribution differed qualitatively across all three paradigm classes: handcrafted radiomics is bimodal with a large excellent-stratum core and a small unstable tail; BiomedCLIP is unimodal-moderate with no catastrophically unstable dimensions; and RadImageNet is broad-spectrum with substantial density across all four reliability bands including a poor-stratum tail of nearly one hundred dimensions. Routine ICC-based stability filtering --- the standard practice in the handcrafted-radiomics community~\cite{pavic2018,vantimmeren2020} --- was confirmed as unnecessary for downstream discrimination in radiomics but was found to confer a small consistent benefit ($+0.014$ to $+0.023$ AUC) and improved probability calibration for both foundation-model paradigms.

The apparent aggregate four-paradigm AUC hierarchy (PyRadiomics $\sim$0.95 $>$ BiomedCLIP 0.898 $>$ RadImageNet 0.794) in our cohort was substantially attributable to a structural size-malignancy confound endemic to LIDC-IDRI; within the matched-size 10--20~mm stratum the ordering persisted with smaller absolute gaps, and within the matched-size 6--10~mm stratum three of the four paradigms converged to AUCs around 0.77 with overlapping confidence intervals. Future foundation-model-versus-radiomics benchmarks should report per-dimension stability metrics, size-stratified AUC, and probability calibration alongside aggregate discrimination, and ICC-based dimension selection should be considered for any foundation-model deployment pipeline that produces probability outputs.

A methodological observation accompanies the substantive findings. The integration of RadImageNet surfaced a silent state-dict load failure mode in the PyTorch port that, undetected, would have produced an apparently publishable but artefactual result. The dual structural-and-functional validation procedure that caught the bug --- explicit key normalisation with \texttt{strict=True} structural verification, plus per-layer magnitude assertions and a cosine-separability check between embeddings of two different test inputs --- is transferable to any downstream study loading pretrained foundation-model weights from a community port, and is increasingly necessary as preprint servers and journals tighten their integrity standards around hallucinated or unverified content~\cite{singh2026arxiv}.
