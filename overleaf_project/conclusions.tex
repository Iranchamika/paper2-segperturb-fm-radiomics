Neither of the two pretrained foundation-model paradigms tested (BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip}; RadImageNet ResNet50~\cite{mei2022radimagenet}) beat IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomic features on either perturbation stability or downstream malignancy discrimination in this LIDC-IDRI cohort. The shape of the per-feature stability distribution differed across the three paradigm classes: handcrafted radiomics was bimodal, with a large excellent-stratum core and a small unstable tail; BiomedCLIP was unimodal, sitting in the moderate-to-good band with no catastrophically unstable dimensions; RadImageNet was broad, spread across all four reliability bands and carrying a poor-stratum tail of nearly one hundred dimensions. Routine ICC-based stability filtering --- the standard practice in the handcrafted-radiomics community~\cite{pavic2018,vantimmeren2020} --- was unnecessary for downstream discrimination in radiomics but gave a small consistent lift ($+0.014$ to $+0.023$ AUC) and improved probability calibration for both foundation-model paradigms.

The aggregate AUC hierarchy in our cohort (PyRadiomics $\sim$0.95 $>$ BiomedCLIP 0.898 $>$ RadImageNet 0.794) was largely driven by a structural size-malignancy correlation endemic to LIDC-IDRI. Within the 10--20~mm stratum the same ordering held with smaller absolute gaps, and within the 6--10~mm stratum three of the four paradigms converged to AUCs around 0.77 with overlapping confidence intervals. Future FM-versus-radiomics benchmarks should report per-dimension stability metrics, size-stratified AUC, and probability calibration alongside aggregate discrimination, and ICC-based dimension selection should be considered for any FM deployment pipeline that produces probability outputs.

A methodological observation accompanies the substantive findings. The RadImageNet integration surfaced a silent state-dict load failure in the PyTorch port that, undetected, would have produced an artefactual result. The structural-plus-functional validation procedure that caught it --- explicit key normalisation with \texttt{strict=True} structural verification, plus per-layer magnitude assertions and a cosine-separability check between embeddings of two different test inputs --- generalises to any downstream study that loads pretrained FM weights from a community port, and is increasingly necessary as preprint servers and journals tighten policies around hallucinated or unverified content~\cite{singh2026arxiv}.
