\subsection{Cohort}

The inclusion cascade screened 1\,018 LIDC-IDRI patient series~\cite{armato2011} via the pylidc-clustered annotation interface~\cite{hancock2016pylidc}; 600 patient series were downloaded from TCIA~\cite{clark2013} across two batches, and 353 annotated nodules met the pre-specified inclusion criteria (Section~2.2). The primary analysis cohort, after seeded balanced downsampling of the malignant class, comprised 310 nodules (155 malignant, 155 benign) drawn from 227 unique patients (Table~1). Benign nodules clustered toward the lower diameter range (median 7.74~mm, IQR 6.70--9.20~mm), and malignant nodules spanned a wider distribution toward larger sizes (median 19.36~mm, IQR 14.30--25.58~mm). This size-class asymmetry is a structural property of LIDC-IDRI and is addressed quantitatively in Section~3.5. Of the 310 cohort nodules, 65\% carried segmentations from all four LIDC readers and 35\% from three. The median pairwise inter-reader Dice similarity coefficient was 0.789 (mean 0.769, range 0.156--0.934), consistent with previously published LIDC inter-reader values of 0.75--0.80~\cite{kalpathycramer2016}, supporting the external validity of the perturbation-magnitude calibration. The per-nodule perturbation-condition count $K$ ranged from 7 to 12 across the 353-nodule extraction superset, reflecting the volume-preservation guard's intentional skipping of erosions that would have retained less than 25\% of the consensus-mask voxel count. The retention rate was 48\% for E1, 13\% for E2, and 2\% for E3; all dilation, translation, and Dice-calibrated perturbations were retained for every nodule.

\subsection{Per-feature stability under segmentation perturbation (H1)}

Across the ten universal perturbation conditions, the four paradigms achieved markedly different proportions of features classified as stable (ICC(2,1) $\geq 0.75$): 94.4\% for PyRadiomics-3D (101 of 107 features), 91.2\% for PyRadiomics-2D (93 of 102), 90.4\% for BiomedCLIP (463 of 512 dimensions), and 75.1\% for RadImageNet ResNet50 (1\,539 of 2\,048 dimensions). Pre-registered two-proportion z-tests across the four foundation-model-versus-radiomics contrasts, Bonferroni-corrected at $\alpha = 0.0125$, yielded the following pattern: BiomedCLIP versus PyRadiomics-2D, $\Delta = -0.8$ percentage points, $z = -0.25$, $p = 0.80$; BiomedCLIP versus PyRadiomics-3D, $\Delta = -4.0$~pp, $z = -1.32$, $p = 0.19$; RadImageNet versus PyRadiomics-2D, $\Delta = -16.1$~pp, $z = -3.69$, $p < 0.001$; RadImageNet versus PyRadiomics-3D, $\Delta = -19.3$~pp, $z = -4.55$, $p < 0.001$. The two RadImageNet contrasts are statistically significant in the direction \emph{opposite} the H1 alternative; the two BiomedCLIP contrasts are non-significant. \textbf{Hypothesis H1 is rejected uniformly across all four contrasts:} neither foundation-model paradigm exceeded either IBSI-aligned radiomics arm on the proportion of perturbation-stable features. RadImageNet was, in fact, the least stable paradigm tested.

\subsection{Distribution-shape divergence (\texorpdfstring{H1$'$}{H1-prime})}

The aggregate-proportion analysis in Section~3.2 hides differences between paradigms that are visible in the ICC stratum crosstab (Figure~2a) and the empirical cumulative distribution functions (Figure~2b). The four paradigms show three different distribution shapes. PyRadiomics is bimodal: 62.6\% of 3D features and 67.6\% of 2D features sit in the excellent stratum (ICC $\geq 0.90$), the rest distribute across good, moderate, and a small unstable tail (one feature in each radiomics arm with ICC $<$ 0.50). BiomedCLIP is unimodal: only 1 of 512 dimensions reaches the excellent stratum, 90.2\% concentrate in the good stratum ($0.75 \leq$ ICC $<$ 0.90), and zero dimensions fall in the poor stratum. RadImageNet ResNet50 spreads broadly: 31.6\% excellent (647 of 2\,048 dimensions), 43.6\% good (892), 20.0\% moderate (410), and 4.8\% poor (98). RadImageNet is the only paradigm with a substantial poor-stratum tail (98 dimensions, against zero for BiomedCLIP and one each for the radiomics arms) and the only paradigm with non-negligible mass in all four reliability bands.

The pre-specified two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests confirm that the original two FM-versus-radiomics contrasts behave as categorically distinct populations of features: BiomedCLIP versus PyRadiomics-2D, $D = 0.722$, $p = 3.2 \times 10^{-44}$; BiomedCLIP versus PyRadiomics-3D, $D = 0.679$, $p = 4.3 \times 10^{-40}$. A secondary comparison between the two PyRadiomics arms yields a small but statistically significant difference ($D = 0.246$, $p = 0.003$), with the 2D arm sitting marginally closer to the excellent stratum than the 3D arm. The three additional K-S contrasts involving RadImageNet (RadImageNet versus PyRadiomics-2D, RadImageNet versus PyRadiomics-3D, and BiomedCLIP versus RadImageNet) are reported in full in Supplementary Table~S6 alongside the original three contrasts, with the test statistics, p-values, and sample sizes computed directly from \texttt{results/icc\_table.csv} by the script in Supplementary File~S8. \textbf{Hypothesis H1$'$ (distribution-shape divergence between paradigms) is strongly supported across every paradigm pair tested}, and the three-way divergence between PyRadiomics' bimodal-excellent, BiomedCLIP's unimodal-good-band, and RadImageNet's wide-spread profiles is reported as the primary novel finding of this study.

\subsection{Downstream classification and the effect of stability filtering (H2, H3)}

Out-of-fold 5-fold stratified cross-validated L2-logistic-regression classifiers~\cite{hosmer2013} achieved AUC values of 0.948 (95\% bootstrap CI 0.920--0.972) for PyRadiomics-3D, 0.955 (0.929--0.976) for PyRadiomics-2D, 0.898 (0.861--0.931) for BiomedCLIP, and 0.794 (full bootstrap CI in Table~2) for RadImageNet ResNet50, using the full unfiltered feature set per paradigm (Figure~3 and Table~2). The four-paradigm hierarchy is therefore PyRadiomics-2D $\approx$ PyRadiomics-3D $>$ BiomedCLIP $>$ RadImageNet on aggregate discrimination, paralleling but not identical to the stability hierarchy in Section~3.2 (PyRadiomics-3D $>$ PyRadiomics-2D $\approx$ BiomedCLIP $>$ RadImageNet).

The pre-versus-post ICC-filter analyses produced two findings worth flagging. \textbf{Neither foundation model showed AUC degradation under stability filtering; both gained slightly.} BiomedCLIP AUC increased by 0.014 at the stricter ICC $\geq 0.85$ threshold despite an 84\% drop in the active feature set ($512 \rightarrow 84$ dimensions). RadImageNet AUC increased by 0.023 at ICC $\geq 0.90$ ($0.794 \rightarrow 0.817$) despite a 68\% drop ($2\,048 \rightarrow 647$ dimensions). Both PyRadiomics arms showed near-zero change at every filter threshold (PyRadiomics-2D $\Delta$AUC within $\pm 0.003$; PyRadiomics-3D within $\pm 0.004$). \textbf{The pre-registered Hypothesis H2, that FM embeddings would show smaller AUC degradation than radiomics under stability filtering, is rejected in its strict form} (no paradigm showed degradation), but the data point to a stronger version of the same idea: ICC-based filtering gives a small but consistent discrimination lift to the foundation models (median $+0.018$ across the two FMs at each paradigm's best threshold), with no equivalent benefit for IBSI-aligned radiomic features in this cohort. \textbf{Hypothesis H3, that post-filter AUC would be non-inferior to pre-filter AUC at margin 0.020, was supported in all four paradigms,} with the two foundation models clearing the bar in the favourable direction by absolute AUC improvements of 0.014 and 0.023. Together, these results extend the finding of Cama~\textit{et al.}~2025~\cite{cama2025} that ICC-based filtering is unnecessary in modern radiomic pipelines from handcrafted to foundation-model features, and refine it: filtering is unnecessary for radiomics in this cohort, mildly beneficial for both FMs.

The calibration metrics revealed an additional unanticipated finding consistent with the discrimination-lift pattern above. BiomedCLIP's unfiltered predictions were poorly calibrated (Brier score 0.144, expected calibration error 0.129), substantially worse than either PyRadiomics arm (Brier 0.074--0.077, ECE 0.030--0.057). Filtering BiomedCLIP to dimensions with ICC $\geq 0.85$ reduced the Brier score by 20\% ($0.144 \rightarrow 0.115$) and the expected calibration error by 50\% ($0.129 \rightarrow 0.065$), with simultaneous improvement in discrimination (AUC $0.898 \rightarrow 0.912$). RadImageNet calibration metrics under the same filter sequence (full-feature versus icc $\geq 0.50$, $0.75$, $0.85$, and $0.90$) are reported in Supplementary Table~S7, computed directly from \texttt{results/downstream\_metrics.csv}. The calibration-improvement effect is consistent with an interpretation in which the moderately-stable foundation-model dimensions encode perturbation-driven noise that adds uncertainty to the probability output without contributing malignancy signal; removing those dimensions tightens the predicted probability distribution toward the true outcome without sacrificing discriminative information.

\subsection{Size-stratified subgroup analysis}

The structural correlation between nodule diameter and malignancy in LIDC (94\% of 6--10~mm nodules benign, 97\% of $>20$~mm nodules malignant) motivated the pre-specified diameter-stratified subgroup analysis. Within the 6--10~mm stratum ($n = 134$; 6\% malignant), three of the four paradigms produced AUC values whose 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals lay entirely above the chance level of 0.5: PyRadiomics-2D 0.774 (CI 0.638--0.896), BiomedCLIP 0.758 (0.611--0.899), and PyRadiomics-3D 0.762 (0.535--0.917, with the lower bound only marginally above chance). The fourth paradigm, RadImageNet ResNet50, produced an AUC of 0.438 (CI 0.254--0.619) whose confidence interval spans 0.5; in the context of the stratum's extreme class imbalance (only 8 malignant nodules of 134 total), this indicates that RadImageNet did not extract usable malignancy signal from very small nodules, in contrast to the other three paradigms which achieved measurable discrimination despite the same class imbalance (Figure~4, left panel). Within the 10--20~mm stratum ($n = 99$; 74\% malignant), the four-paradigm hierarchy from the aggregate analysis persists: PyRadiomics-3D 0.889 (CI 0.805--0.960), BiomedCLIP 0.794 (0.697--0.884), and RadImageNet 0.677 (0.565--0.793), with PyRadiomics-3D and BiomedCLIP both significantly above chance and RadImageNet trailing both by 0.12 and 0.21 AUC respectively, although the RadImageNet-versus-BiomedCLIP confidence intervals overlap and the difference is not formally significant at this sample size (Figure~4, middle panel). The $>20$~mm stratum was not evaluable for any paradigm because the LIDC corpus contained only three benign nodules in this size range, a structural consequence of Lung-RADS' clinical convention of treating $>20$~mm solid nodules as suspicious by definition~\cite{macmahon2017} (Figure~4, right panel).

\textbf{The aggregate four-paradigm AUC hierarchy (PyRadiomics $\sim$0.95 $>$ BiomedCLIP 0.898 $>$ RadImageNet 0.794) is largely driven by the structural size-malignancy correlation in LIDC rather than by a uniform paradigm-level feature-quality ranking,} but the within-stratum analysis shows the ranking is not pure artefact. Within the 10--20~mm stratum the same ordering holds with smaller absolute gaps. Within the 6--10~mm stratum three of the four paradigms cluster around AUC 0.76, while RadImageNet does not discriminate. The methodological implications for future FM-versus-radiomics benchmarking, that aggregate AUC comparisons must be reported alongside size-stratified subgroup analyses to separate paradigm-level differences from corpus-structural confounds, are taken up in Section~4.4. The interpretive limits of the 6--10~mm RadImageNet result, given the stratum's 6\% positive rate, are discussed in Section~4.5.
