\subsection{Summary of findings}

We compared how segmentation-mask perturbation propagates through two pretrained foundation-model embeddings (BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip}; RadImageNet ResNet50~\cite{mei2022radimagenet}) and IBSI-aligned handcrafted radiomic features~\cite{vangriethuysen2017,zwanenburg2020} extracted in matched 2D and 3D configurations from the same lung-CT nodule cohort (LIDC-IDRI~\cite{armato2011}; 310 balanced nodules with up to four-reader natural segmentation variability per nodule, augmented by seven controlled morphological perturbations and one Dice-calibrated synthetic mask per nodule). The four pre-specified hypotheses produced a mixed picture across four paradigm-pair contrasts. The hypothesis that foundation-model embeddings would show a higher proportion of stable (ICC $\geq 0.75$) features than handcrafted radiomics was rejected for every contrast: PyRadiomics-3D reached 94.4\% stable features, PyRadiomics-2D 91.2\%, BiomedCLIP 90.4\%, and RadImageNet ResNet50 75.1\%, the lowest of the four. The ICC distributions had three different shapes: radiomics was bimodal with a large excellent-stratum mode and a small unstable tail; BiomedCLIP was unimodal and sat in the moderate-to-good stratum with no poor (ICC $<$ 0.50) dimensions; RadImageNet spread across all four reliability bands and carried a poor-stratum tail of 98 dimensions. The downstream classification analysis showed that ICC-based stability filtering had near-zero effect on radiomics AUC, lifted both FM AUCs by roughly 0.02 (BiomedCLIP $+0.014$ at ICC $\geq 0.85$; RadImageNet $+0.023$ at ICC $\geq 0.90$), and improved BiomedCLIP calibration substantially. The diameter-stratified subgroup analysis showed that the aggregate four-paradigm hierarchy (PyRadiomics $\sim$0.95 $>$ BiomedCLIP 0.898 $>$ RadImageNet 0.794) was largely a size-malignancy confound endemic to LIDC: within the 6--10~mm stratum, three of the four paradigms converged to AUCs around 0.77 with overlapping confidence intervals, while RadImageNet did not extract usable signal (AUC 0.438, CI 0.254--0.619 spanning chance). During the RadImageNet integration we caught a silent state-dict load failure in the PyTorch port that, undetected, would have shipped an artefact as a finding; the dual structural-and-functional validation procedure that caught it is reported in Section~4.6 alongside the substantive results.

\subsection{Comparison to the peer-reviewed literature}

The peer-reviewed precedent for foundation-model stability analysis in cancer imaging is Pai~\textit{et al.}~2024~\cite{pai2024}, who introduced FMCIB, a three-dimensional contrastively pretrained ResNet50, and reported ICC = 0.984 for its feature-based predictions on the RIDER same-day repeat-scan dataset, with significantly higher stability than a supervised CNN baseline under three-dimensional Gaussian seed-point jitter ($\sigma^2 = 16$ voxels per dimension) on the LUNG1 cohort. Pai~\textit{et al.}~2024 established that domain-pretrained 3D foundation models can be more stable than supervised CNN baselines under scanner-repeat and seed-point variability. The present study extends this peer-reviewed evidence base in three concrete directions. First, the seed-point-jitter perturbation is replaced with controlled segmentation-mask perturbation, which is the dominant noise source in handcrafted radiomic pipelines and the variability mode that radiomics practitioners actually filter for via ICC~\cite{pavic2018,vantimmeren2020}. Second, an IBSI-aligned PyRadiomics comparator is extracted from the same lesions under the same perturbations, enabling a true head-to-head paradigm comparison rather than an FM-internal benchmark. Third, ICC is computed per dimension rather than as a single-output statistic across the embedding, which reveals the distribution-shape divergence (Section~3.3) that single-output stability statistics necessarily conceal. A contemporaneous preprint by the same group extends the FMCIB benchmark to ten 3D foundation models using global cosine similarity; this work has not undergone peer review and is referenced here only for completeness, without being treated as established precedent. Cama~\textit{et al.}~2025~\cite{cama2025}, working independently on breast MRI for triple-negative breast cancer subtype prediction, reported the counterintuitive finding that ICC-based filtering of radiomic features can exclude features with predictive value; the H3 result reported here extends and corroborates that finding for both handcrafted radiomics and foundation-model embeddings on lung CT.

\subsection{Mechanistic interpretation of the distribution-shape divergence}

The most striking finding of this study is the difference in ICC distribution shape across the four paradigms, which fall into three distinct patterns (Kolmogorov--Smirnov $D = 0.72$, $p < 10^{-43}$ between BiomedCLIP and PyRadiomics-2D being the largest of the original three pairwise contrasts; the three additional contrasts involving RadImageNet are reported in Supplementary Table~S6). Three mechanistic explanations are consistent with this pattern. First, the SimCLR-style contrastive pretraining objective underlying BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip} enforces approximately uniform invariance to image augmentations across every embedding dimension: the loss function rewards the encoder for producing nearly identical vectors for two augmented views of the same image, and this pressure operates uniformly across all 512 dimensions of the output. The empirical consequence is a unimodal distribution concentrated in the moderate-to-good stability band with no catastrophically unstable dimensions. Second, IBSI radiomic features have heterogeneous mathematical sensitivities to mask perturbation by construction~\cite{zwanenburg2020}: features such as \texttt{firstorder\_Mean}, \texttt{firstorder\_TotalEnergy}, \texttt{shape\_VoxelVolume}, and \texttt{shape\_MeshVolume} are mathematically near-invariant to small boundary perturbations, whereas certain GLSZM, GLDM, and high-frequency wavelet-derived texture features are inherently sensitive to mask edges and produce the small unstable tail in the radiomics distribution. The bimodal radiomics distribution thus reflects the underlying bimodality of the feature definitions: a robust core of intensity-and-shape descriptors plus a noise-sensitive periphery of high-order texture descriptors. Third, RadImageNet ResNet50~\cite{mei2022radimagenet} was pretrained on a supervised multi-class classification task over 1.35 million annotated CT, MRI, and ultrasound images across 11 anatomies and 165 labels, with no explicit invariance objective in the loss function. The empirical consequence is a wide stability distribution in which different convolutional filters acquire different perturbation sensitivities depending on the local image statistics each filter learns to detect: early-layer filters that pick up low-frequency intensity gradients are mask-robust, while later-layer filters that pick up fine textural variation are mask-sensitive. The 98 RadImageNet dimensions in the poor-stratum tail are without parallel in either BiomedCLIP (zero poor) or radiomics (one poor in each arm) and represent a distinct mode of perturbation-driven instability specific to supervised-pretrained convolutional encoders. The three paradigms differ not primarily in \emph{how much} stability they provide, but in \emph{which dimensions} carry the stability and in the shape of the per-dimension stability distribution, a methodologically important distinction that aggregate proportion-stable metrics conceal.

A second mechanistic observation arises from the calibration analysis (Section~3.4). BiomedCLIP's raw 512-dimensional embeddings produced poorly calibrated probability outputs (Brier score 0.144, expected calibration error 0.129) even at strong discrimination (AUC 0.898). ICC-based filtering to the 84 dimensions with ICC $\geq 0.85$ reduced both Brier and ECE by $\geq 20$\% while simultaneously improving discrimination. This is consistent with an interpretation in which the moderately-stable BiomedCLIP dimensions encode perturbation-driven noise that adds calibration uncertainty without contributing malignancy signal: when these dimensions are removed, the L2-regularised logistic regression no longer needs to balance contributions from unstable noise dimensions, and the resulting probability outputs are sharper and better calibrated. To our knowledge this calibration-improvement effect under per-dimension ICC selection has not been previously reported for foundation-model embeddings, and it is clinically actionable for any deployment pipeline that reports probability outputs rather than ranked predictions.

\subsection{Size-confound finding and implications for the FM-radiomics benchmarking literature}

The size-stratified subgroup analysis (Section~3.5; Figure~4) is, in our assessment, the most methodologically consequential finding of this study. The unfiltered aggregate AUC ranking, handcrafted radiomics at 0.95 versus BiomedCLIP at 0.90, was substantially driven by the structural correlation between nodule diameter and malignancy in LIDC-IDRI, rather than by paradigm-level feature-quality differences. Handcrafted radiomic features include explicit shape descriptors (\texttt{shape\_VoxelVolume}, \texttt{shape\_MaximumDiameter}, \texttt{shape\_MeshVolume}) that encode lesion size directly~\cite{vangriethuysen2017,zwanenburg2020}. The BiomedCLIP preprocessing pipeline used here, bounding-box cropping with 50\% padding followed by resampling to $224 \times 224$ pixels, normalises absolute size out of the input by design, as is standard for vision-transformer inference. The aggregate AUC comparison therefore confounded ``radiomics has explicit size features'' with ``radiomics produces higher-quality features for malignancy classification.'' Within the matched-size 6--10~mm stratum, the AUC gap dissolved to within the 95\% confidence-interval overlap (0.762 / 0.774 / 0.758 for the three paradigms); within the matched-size 10--20~mm stratum a residual $\sim 0.095$ AUC advantage for PyRadiomics-3D persisted with overlapping confidence intervals, plausibly reflecting genuine sub-stratum information in three-dimensional shape descriptors that the normalised single-slice BiomedCLIP input cannot recover.

The methodological implication for the wider FM-versus-radiomics benchmarking literature is direct: any benchmark study performed on a cohort with structural size-class imbalance will systematically inflate handcrafted radiomics' apparent advantage relative to foundation models with size-normalising preprocessing. Future FM-radiomics comparisons should report size-stratified AUC values as the primary discrimination metric and treat aggregate AUC as a confounded secondary statistic. The apparent heterogeneity of published FM-versus-radiomics comparisons across cohorts may, in substantial part, be a consequence of unmeasured size-class composition differences across those cohorts.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations of this work should be acknowledged. First, although two foundation-model paradigms were included (BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip} and RadImageNet ResNet50~\cite{mei2022radimagenet}), the contemporary foundation-model landscape includes many additional architectures and pretraining regimes that this study did not test; generalisation to 3D models such as FMCIB~\cite{pai2024}, to segmentation-pretrained models such as MedSAM~\cite{ma2024medsam}, and to more recent vision-transformer-large or masked-autoencoder pretraining strategies remains an open question for which a multi-FM extension is planned. Second, the entire study was performed on a single anatomical site (lung), a single modality (computed tomography), and a single cohort (LIDC-IDRI); cross-site, cross-modality, and cross-cohort generalisation are deferred to a planned external-validation study. Third, the 6~mm diameter floor reflects a pre-registered fallback cascade from the originally pre-registered 10~mm floor~\cite{macmahon2017}; the sensitivity analysis at 10~mm preserves protocol-fidelity reporting but reduces $N$ to 129 with substantial loss of statistical power. Fourth, the $>20$~mm stratum was not evaluable due to LIDC's structural scarcity of large benign nodules, a corpus-design limitation rather than an analytical one. Fifth, the 6--10~mm RadImageNet within-stratum AUC (0.438; CI 0.254--0.619) must be interpreted with caution: the stratum contains only 8 malignant nodules in 134 total (6\% positive rate), and the confidence interval spans the chance level of 0.5. The interpretive conclusion drawn in Section~3.5, that RadImageNet did not extract usable malignancy signal from very small nodules, rests on the contrast with the other three paradigms (PyRadiomics-2D 0.774, BiomedCLIP 0.758, PyRadiomics-3D 0.762) which achieved confidence intervals lying entirely above chance under the same class imbalance; it should not be read as a paradigm-level claim until replicated on a cohort with a more balanced small-nodule subgroup. In particular, the combination of a 2\,048-dimensional embedding space and only 8 positive training cases within this stratum creates a dimensionality-to-positive-case ratio that standard L2 regularisation may be unable to constrain; the observed failure may therefore reflect catastrophic overfitting rather than an inherent architectural inability to perceive small-nodule malignancy features. Sixth, all feature extraction was performed on CPU; the foundation-model two-dimensional inference protocol was chosen partly for this constraint, and a three-dimensional FM extension on GPU-enabled compute is a natural follow-on. Seventh, the L2-regularised logistic regression chosen as the downstream classifier~\cite{hosmer2013} was selected for its bit-exact reproducibility properties and its standard use in the radiomics ICC-filter literature; alternative classifiers (gradient-boosting, random forests, regularised survival models) may exhibit different sensitivities to feature-set composition. Eighth, the static-ICC pre-filter selection mechanism (Section~2.5.2) was a documented protocol deviation from the originally pre-registered nested-CV form; the planned response at revision time, if reviewers request it, is to implement the fully nested form and report it as a sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{Validation procedure for community-distributed foundation-model weights}

The two-foundation-model arm of this study surfaced a class of failure mode that, although simple, matters as foundation-model checkpoints are redistributed through community ports rather than published as part of the original architecture release. During the integration of RadImageNet ResNet50~\cite{mei2022radimagenet}, an initial pipeline run produced an apparently striking finding: 1\,724 of 2\,048 dimensions (84\%) reached the Koo and Li excellent reliability threshold (ICC $\geq$ 0.90), paired with a downstream AUC of 0.747 and a 6--10~mm within-stratum AUC of 0.384, a pattern that, taken at face value, would have supported a narrative of perturbation stability decoupled from task informativeness. A pre-commit audit revealed the cause: the RadImageNet PyTorch port stores weights in an \texttt{nn.Sequential} indexing convention (\texttt{backbone.0.*} through \texttt{backbone.7.*}) rather than torchvision's named-attribute convention (\texttt{conv1.*}, \texttt{bn1.*}, \texttt{layer1.*} through \texttt{layer4.*}). The standard PyTorch idiom \texttt{model.load\_state\_dict(state, strict=False)}, routinely used to tolerate missing classification-head keys when the head is replaced with \texttt{nn.Identity} for feature extraction, silently accepted the complete key mismatch as a no-operation load, leaving the network running inference with PyTorch's default Kaiming-uniform random initialisation. The 84\%-excellent-ICC pattern was an artefact of random features producing deterministically consistent outputs under deterministic preprocessing.

The fix is structurally simple: an explicit key-rename map applied to the saved state-dict before load, plus an upgrade from \texttt{strict=False} to \texttt{strict=True} so that any remaining structural mismatch fails loudly rather than silently. Two additional functional checks confirm trained-weight behaviour beyond what structural verification alone can guarantee: a per-layer magnitude-floor assertion (\texttt{conv1.weight.abs().mean() > 0.05}) that catches random-init residuals, and a cosine-similarity check between embeddings of two different test images that distinguishes a discriminating encoder (cosine similarity well below 1) from a collapsed encoder (cosine similarity at unity). For RadImageNet, the correctly-loaded encoder produced cosine similarity 0.997 between two LIDC nodule embeddings, compared with 0.9998 for the random-init baseline and 0.992 for ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 used as a positive control, a quantitative signature that distinguished the trained from the untrained state when neither the structural load nor the magnitude check alone would have been definitive.

The lesson is that any downstream study loading pretrained foundation-model weights from a community port whose key conventions may diverge from the architecture's reference implementation must apply both structural verification (\texttt{strict=True} after explicit key normalisation) and functional verification (depth-magnitude profile plus cosine-separability of test inputs) before drawing conclusions from downstream metrics. The 2025--2026 tightening of preprint-server and journal policies around hallucinated or unverified content~\cite{singh2026arxiv} makes this discipline a practical necessity, particularly as the foundation-model ecosystem continues to fragment across architecture-port pairs in which silent state-dict mismatches are an under-recognised failure mode.

\subsection{Future work}

The most natural next extension is a three-dimensional foundation-model arm: although both BiomedCLIP and RadImageNet ResNet50 operate on two-dimensional axial slices, the segmentation-perturbation framework developed here generalises directly to volumetric architectures including FMCIB~\cite{pai2024}, MedSAM~\cite{ma2024medsam} adapted to whole-volume inputs, and recent vision-transformer-large or masked-autoencoder pretraining strategies, on GPU-enabled compute. A multi-cohort external-validation extension to NSCLC-Radiomics, NSCLC-Radiogenomics, and an Australian rural-cohort dataset would address the cross-site generalisation limitation identified in Section~4.5. The within-paradigm calibration-improvement effect observed for BiomedCLIP and RadImageNet under per-dimension ICC selection warrants a dedicated study testing whether the effect is general across contrastively-pretrained and supervised-pretrained foundation-model families respectively, and whether it transfers to alternative downstream classifiers beyond L2-regularised logistic regression. The 6--10~mm RadImageNet within-stratum result (Section~3.5) similarly invites replication on a cohort with a more balanced small-nodule subgroup, to determine whether RadImageNet's apparent failure to discriminate at small nodule sizes is paradigm-specific or class-imbalance-driven. Finally, the dual structural-and-functional validation procedure proposed in Section~4.6 should be packaged as a small open-source utility and applied to additional foundation-model community ports, building a public registry of known weight-load conventions for downstream-study reproducibility.
